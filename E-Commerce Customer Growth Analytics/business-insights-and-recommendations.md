# Business Insights & Recommendations

## Executive Findings

### 1. Repeat purchasing is the primary growth gap

The delivered customer base contains **93,358 customers**, but only **2,801 are repeat customers**, producing an overall repeat rate of approximately **3.00%**. The average customer places about **1.03 delivered orders**.

**Business implication:** revenue growth is strongly dependent on acquiring new customers. Improving first-to-second-order conversion is likely to be a major retention opportunity.

### 2. High-value one-time customers are the biggest reactivation opportunity

The **High-Value One-Time** segment contains **21,623 customers** and represents approximately **7.33M** in customer value.

**Business implication:** the company already has a large audience that has demonstrated purchasing capacity but has not returned. This segment is a strong candidate for targeted reactivation.

### 3. Champions are the most engaged segment

The **Champions** segment contains **6,824 customers** with a repeat rate of approximately **14.45%**, materially above the overall 3% benchmark.

**Business implication:** these customers demonstrate stronger engagement and should be protected through loyalty and personalized retention initiatives.

### 4. Customer value is concentrated across segments

High-Value One-Time customers contribute approximately **7.33M**, while Champions contribute approximately **1.34M** and Loyal Customers approximately **1.51M**.

**Business implication:** customer strategy should not rely on one generic campaign. Different segments require different retention and reactivation treatments.

### 5. Cohort retention falls sharply after the first purchase

Cohort analysis validates 100% retention in month 0 and shows very low retention in subsequent months, with many month-1 cohort rates below 1%.

**Business implication:** the period immediately following the first purchase is critical for converting a first-time customer into a repeat customer.

### 6. Category behavior provides a useful segmentation signal

Repeat behavior varies across product categories. Statistical testing identified category as the strongest association among the tested first-order retention drivers, but the effect size remained relatively small (**Cramér's V ≈ 0.067**).

**Business implication:** category is useful for targeting and experimentation, but category alone should not be treated as the cause of retention.

## Statistical Evidence

| Driver | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| First-order value | Mann-Whitney p ≈ 0.00489; rank-biserial ≈ -0.031 | Statistically significant, but very small effect |
| Delivery outcome | Chi-square p ≈ 0.0301; Cramér's V ≈ 0.011 | Very weak association |
| First-order value band | Chi-square p ≈ 0.00976; Cramér's V ≈ 0.013 | Very weak association |
| Review grouping | p ≈ 0.2397 | Not statistically significant |
| Product category | Chi-square p ≈ 1.58e-51; Cramér's V ≈ 0.067 | Strongest tested association, but still small |

These tests support prioritization and experimentation, not causal conclusions.

## Recommended Business Actions

### Priority 1 — Convert first-time customers into second-time customers

- Build a post-purchase lifecycle journey.
- Trigger personalized recommendations after delivery.
- Test a second-order incentive with controlled discounting.
- Measure **first-to-second-order conversion** as a primary KPI.

### Priority 2 — Reactivate High-Value One-Time customers

- Create a dedicated reactivation campaign.
- Use the customer's first purchase category to recommend complementary products.
- Test personalized offers against a non-discount control group.

### Priority 3 — Protect Champions and Loyal Customers

- Introduce loyalty benefits and early-access experiences.
- Use inactivity triggers before customers become dormant.
- Personalize recommendations based on historical purchase behavior.

### Priority 4 — Use category-specific retention strategies

- Identify categories with above-average repeat behavior.
- Test cross-sell recommendations from low-repeat categories into complementary categories.
- Compare category-specific campaigns using repeat conversion rather than campaign clicks alone.

### Priority 5 — Establish a retention KPI framework

Track the following monthly:

- Repeat Customer Rate
- First-to-Second-Order Conversion
- Cohort Month-1 and Month-3 Retention
- Revenue from Repeat Customers
- Customer Value by RFM Segment
- Reactivation Rate of High-Value One-Time Customers

## Expected Strategic Direction

The analysis suggests a shift from a primarily **acquisition-led growth model** toward a more balanced **acquisition + retention model**. The biggest near-term opportunity is not simply acquiring more customers, but increasing the probability that existing first-time customers make a second purchase.
